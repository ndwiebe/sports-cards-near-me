# How close is any city to being sellable?

`PLAN.md` step 9 sets the rule in one sentence: **"if you cannot show the shop the number, you
cannot sell the slot."** Concretely a city is not offered a paid slot until its own page has:

1. **90 days of Search Console history** (Search Console is Google's free report on what people
   searched to find you), and
2. roughly **100+ appearances in search results per month**, and
3. **20+ outbound taps per month** — someone tapping Directions or Call and actually reaching the
   business.

Measured 2026-09-04 against the real export (`gsc-export-2026-09-03`, a 36-day window) and the live
click counter.

## Where the three conditions actually stand

| Condition | Status |
|---|---|
| 90 days of history | **Not yet.** The useful data starts 2026-07-30, so the earliest any city clears this is roughly **late October 2026**. |
| 100+ appearances/month | **Already cleared by 6 cities comfortably**, 11 in total over the window. |
| 20+ outbound taps/month | **Nowhere close, and this is the binding constraint.** Site-wide the counter has recorded **7 taps in total** since it went live on 2026-08-28. |

## The cities carrying the traffic

Appearances and visits over the 36-day window:

| City | Appearances | Visits |
|---|---|---|
| London, ON | 2,241 | 17 |
| Toronto, ON | 1,972 | 14 |
| Ottawa, ON | 1,903 | 18 |
| Mississauga, ON | 1,197 | 6 |
| Edmonton, AB | 745 | 7 |
| Markham, ON | 734 | 9 |

180 of 249 city pages appear in search at all; together they earned 13,064 appearances and 162
visits over the window.

## What this actually means

**The appearance half of the bar is solved.** Six cities are already well past 100/month, and London
— not Toronto — is the strongest, which nobody predicted.

**The tap half is the whole problem.** A city page needs 20 outbound taps a month; the entire site
has produced 7 since launch. Two things bear on that, and only one is a traffic problem:

- Until 2026-09-04, **"Call" was not a button** — it was a plain phone number in a details list,
  while Directions was a large gradient button. 524 shops have a phone. That is now fixed, so the
  next 90 days measure a fairer version of the question than the last week did.
- The rest is genuine volume. ~217 visits a week across 1,463 pages does not concentrate enough
  people onto any single city page to produce 20 deliberate taps from it.

**The honest read: the binding constraint on monetisation is not the gate, the paperwork, or the
pricing — it is outbound taps, and the counter is now measuring the right thing for the first time.**
Re-check this file when the 90-day mark lands in late October, using the same two sources.

## What would change the answer fastest

Ranked by effect on taps per city page, not on traffic in the abstract:

1. **The six cities above are where any effort pays.** They already have the audience; they are
   short the action.
2. **Shop-name searches are 40–47% of all clicks** and they land on *shop* pages, not city pages.
   Those visitors are the most likely to tap — they searched for one specific business. A shop page
   converting to a tap helps that shop, but does not move a *city* page toward its bar.
3. Nothing here argues for more city pages. 146 of 248 already have exactly one shop, and the
   2026-08-27 research flagged thin city pages as a live doorway-page risk.
