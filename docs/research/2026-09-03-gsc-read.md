# Monthly Search Console read — 2026-09-03

**What this is:** "Search Console" is Google's free dashboard that shows how sportscardsnearme.ca
shows up in Google search results — how often it appears (impressions), how often people click
(clicks), what percentage of appearances turn into a click (CTR — click-through rate), and where
in the results list it lands (average position, where 1 is the very top). This read pulls that
data automatically and compares it to the last read (2026-08-28) and the site's goal date of
5 November.

Data pulled cleanly — no authentication problems. Raw export:
`docs/research/gsc-export-2026-09-03/`. Comparison baseline: `docs/research/gsc-export-2026-08-28/`.

Everything below is a small dataset — the whole site earns a few hundred clicks a week. Anywhere
a number is built on single-digit counts, it's flagged as a direction, not a hard measurement.

---

## 1. Average position — split by device (the honest scoreboard)

Google reports one blended "average position" number, but that blend mixes together phone
visitors and desktop visitors, who behave very differently. The site's own investigation on
2026-08-28 found desktop's poor ranking has **no fixable cause** — five explanations were tested
and ruled out — so it's dead weight that drags the blended number down for no reason anyone can
act on. **Mobile is the honest scoreboard**, because ~75% of the site's visits and clicks come
from phones.

| | Blended (all devices) | Mobile only | Desktop only |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2026-08-28** | 10.65 | 8.30 | 17.64 |
| **2026-09-03** | 10.04 | 8.12 | 16.14 |
| **Change** | −0.61 (better) | −0.18 (better) | −1.50 (better) |

**Goal: average position ≤ 7.5.** Mobile is now **8.12** — 0.62 off the goal, closer than a
week ago. Both numbers moved the right direction; desktop even improved somewhat, though it's
still deep on page two and not worth chasing without a cause.

One note on the numbers themselves: the task brief for this read cited "~9.4 blended / 8.34
mobile" as the 2026-08-28 baseline. Those figures actually come from two different, older
snapshots (a PRD trend table from 2026-08-07, and a diagnosis note from 2026-08-25) — not from
the 2026-08-28 export itself. Running the actual analysis tool against the actual 2026-08-28
export file gives 10.65 blended / 8.30 mobile, which is what's used above for an apples-to-apples
comparison. The direction of travel (mobile improving, still short of goal) is the same either
way.

## 3. Did the grading-guide retitle work? — Yes, confirmed: the page is the problem, not the title

Background: the site's grading guide (`/guides/card-grading-companies-canada/`) had its title
changed on 2026-08-28 to include "Beckett" — a top grading-company brand name that was ranking
well but earning almost no clicks. The recorded hypothesis was: if Beckett-related searches still
earn nothing even from a strong position, the title wasn't the problem — the page content is.

The data says the hypothesis is **confirmed**. Looking at every query containing "Beckett":

| Query | Position 08-28 | Position 09-03 | Clicks (both weeks) |
|---|---|---|---|
| beckett card grading | **2** | **2** | 0 |
| beckett grading canada | 8.7 | 9.1 | 0 |
| beckett grading | 7.1 | 13.3 | 0 |
| beckett grading canada locations | 9.9 | 10.1 | 0 |
| does beckett grade in canada | 11.0 | 10.6 | 0 |
| beckett canada | 12.3 | 12.3 | 0 |
| beckett card grading (misc.) | — | — | 1 click / 1 impression (statistically meaningless) |

Across roughly 100 impressions of genuinely Beckett-branded searches, at positions ranging from
**2** (top of page one) down to 13, the page earned essentially **zero real clicks** in both
weeks. A result sitting at position 2 with zero clicks out of 5 impressions isn't a fluke of small
numbers alone — it's a strong signal the search snippet (the title/description Google shows) or
the page content itself isn't giving searchers what they're looking for, even when Google is
already showing it to them near the top.

The page's overall numbers did tick up (12 → 15 clicks, 1,033 → 1,333 impressions, median position
15.5 → 14.6), but that's driven by other, non-Beckett queries on the page — not by the retitle
doing its job. **Conclusion: the retitle didn't fix the underlying problem. The next step, if this
page is worth more investment, is rewriting the page content/snippet, not the title.**

## 4. Shop-name queries — still the strongest finding, still growing

Matching every search query against the site's actual list of ~670 shop names confirms this is
still true: people search for specific card shops by name far more than by city.

| | 08-28 | 09-03 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total site clicks | 70 | 121 | +73% |
| Clicks from shop-name searches | 33 | 49 | **+48%** |
| Share of clicks that are shop-name searches | 47% | 40% | (down slightly, but still ~2 in 5 clicks) |

Shop-name clicks are growing in raw numbers, just not quite as fast as total clicks — so the
share dipped a little, but shop names remain the single biggest driver of clicks on the whole
site. One shop dominates:

- **D&M Sports (Cards)** — by far the top performer: 24 clicks / 2,352 impressions on 08-28 →
  **39 clicks / 2,929 impressions** on 09-03. This one shop alone accounts for roughly a third of
  all clicks on the entire site.
- **HK Collectibles** — 2 → 3 clicks, ~800-830 impressions both weeks.
- A long tail of ~120 other shop names generating impressions with 0–1 clicks each — the kind of
  small-number noise that's a direction (people are searching, not yet clicking), not a
  measurement.

This remains the strongest, clearest pattern in the data: **the site's real audience is people who
already know a shop's name and are looking it up**, not people browsing by city. That has design
implications (shop pages should be built for someone who already decided) that the city/province
pages don't need to solve.
