# Plan: SCNM monetisation, shop outreach, and the SSC services channel
_Locked via grill — by Claude + Nathan Wiebe, 2026-07-31. Revised after Codex review round 1._

## Goal

Turn Sports Cards Near Me — an independent Canadian card-shop directory of 689 shops across 248 cities, 92% carrying Google ratings — into a business that earns two ways without spending the one asset that makes it work: being the list nobody paid to be on. Money comes from **labelled Featured placement** sold to shops, resellers and show organisers, and from **AI/web/SEO services** sold under Slab Savvy CPA to shops the directory has evidence have a web problem. Accounting and tax work is referred to KRP, which pays a 10% first-year referral fee. The directory's rankings stay unbuyable.

## Approach

1. **Clear G1–G5.**
2. **Fix the public claims** (About + Privacy + FAQ + structured data) *before* anything is sold.
3. **Define typed placement data** — not a boolean. Placement type, surface, UTC start/end dates, term, and disclosure flag. Store placements as **separate sanitised placement records keyed by placement ID + an IMMUTABLE store ID — never the slug**. Slugs are generated from `name + city` (`stores-build.ts:24`) and then de-duplicated **by row order** (`:44-49`), so a rename, a move, a row reorder, or removing a duplicate silently reassigns which business holds a given slug. This is not theoretical: deduplicating rows on 2026-07-30 changed exactly these slugs. Keying paid placements or analytics to a slug means an active ad can silently repoint to a different shop. Add a stable ID column, key everything to it, and treat the slug as a mutable URL field only — fields hung off a Store row cannot represent current, future and historical bookings at once. v1 supports `kind: store` only; other listing kinds are added when their surfaces exist.
   **Slot caps are an interval problem, not a key-match problem.** Keying on an exact date range lets two overlapping-but-not-identical bookings both pass. The rule: reject any booking that would cause concurrent active placements for a `province + city + placement type` to exceed the cap **at any instant within its interval**. Bake-time validation enforces this and fails the build on violation.
4. **v1 is SHOPS ONLY, on city pages.** Featured is sold to shops and nobody else in v1. City routes are generated from Stores, contain no Shows, and some Resellers have no store-backed city page — so selling Shows or Resellers placement in v1 would be selling a surface that does not exist. Shows and Resellers placement is deferred until their own eligibility and rendering are defined. The v1 surface is one separate, clearly-labelled module on city pages sitting *above an unchanged organic list*. Maps, rankings, guides, province pages and organic JSON-LD are explicitly out. "Never reorders organic results" is only enforceable if the paid surface is separate — as originally written it was not implementable.
5. **Verify the "featured ring" exists** before relying on it. It is currently only in a year-old design spec, not in shipped components. Build an accessible Featured component whose label reads clearly **without colour**, tested at 375px and desktop.
6. **Qualify paid links.** `rel="sponsored noopener"` on every advertiser-controlled outbound link, tested on each paid surface, per Google's outbound-link qualification guidance.
7. **Sell through a reservation ledger with states, not just a manual invoice.** Sending an invoice does not reserve anything — a second buyer can be invoiced for the same capacity while the first invoice is unpaid, and both then pay. Maintain a **private reservation ledger** with `held / paid / cancelled / expired` states, an expiry on holds, and overlap validation that counts **held AND paid** inventory before any invoice is issued. One generic Stripe link cannot do this; issue a city-and-term-specific invoice against a held reservation and activate only on confirmed payment.
   **Cap = ONE slot per city in v1** (decided 2026-07-31). Not a range. Three reasons: scarcity is the product and one is its strongest form; 1 -> 2 is an expansion you can announce while 3 -> 1 is a downgrade needing refunds; and multiple Featured shops in one city would create an internal paid ordering — which is exactly the paid ranking this plan refuses to sell. Stored in private configuration and asserted in the overlap tests.
8. **Wire real analytics before selling.** Search Console measures Google-to-site clicks, not listing outbound clicks, and the current Cloudflare beacon is generic. Choose one event-capable system, define listing/category/destination events with bot filtering, update the privacy page, and set a minimum traffic window and sales threshold.
9. **The traffic bar, in one sentence: if you cannot show the shop the number, you cannot sell the slot.** (Nathan, 2026-07-31: "this shouldn't roll out until I have traffic I can stand behind.") Concretely — a city is not offered until its own page has **90 days of Search Console history** and clears a floor of roughly **100+ impressions and 20+ outbound listing clicks per month**. Name the analytics provider, the metrics, the window and the bot-exclusion method up front. As of 2026-07-31 there is **one day** of data and six impressions, so the earliest honest sale is roughly three months out; the intervening work is the gated build, not selling.
10. **Run outreach under a CASL procedure.** Cold email in Canada is a commercial electronic message: documented consent basis, sender identification, mailing address, working unsubscribe, and a suppression ledger — in place before contacting any shop.
11. **State outreach evidence honestly.** From `scripts/scan-card-evidence.py` (2026-07-30, calibrated on 15 known card shops: 13 confirmed, 0 false negatives): 181 shops have no website, 24 use a social page as their website, and 57 of a **111-shop flagged subset** had no card terms found on their site. Say *"our scan did not find card terms on your site"* — never *"you are proven invisible to AI"*. Manually verify every shop before contacting it.
12. **Split the work by lane, and get the SHOP's consent before any referral.** AI/web/SEO/GEO/process → Slab Savvy CPA. Tax/accounting/bookkeeping → referred to KRP at 10% of first-year fees. G4 covers Nathan and KRP; it does **not** authorise sharing a shop owner's contact or engagement details with a third party. Explicit lead consent is required before transmitting anything, with proof retained privately. Nathan's standing rule: software and AI only, no accounting or tax on the side.
13. **Price in cash; accept cards as a bounded option.** Barter capped at **25% of the pre-tax invoice**, valued from named sources at deal time in writing, with authentication, condition and title recorded, and **taxes always settled in cash**. Never "unpriced".
14. **Write the editorial-independence policy BEFORE the first client, not after.** A disclosure marker is not a control. The policy must prohibit, in writing: ranking intervention of any kind, preferential handling of corrections, review or rating manipulation, and unequal data-refresh treatment for clients — with an auditable exception process and a named reason recorded for any deviation. Disclose services clients on their listing with contractual consent, and add tests proving relationship fields cannot reach any ranking function.
15. **Monitor expiry.** Explicit UTC start/end dates, alerting on stale or failed builds — a failed daily build otherwise leaves expired ads live or paid ads missing indefinitely — plus contractual activation delays and make-goods.
16. **Acceptance tests before launch:** slot caps, expired placements, label visibility without colour, `rel="sponsored"`, ranking invariance under relationship fields, click events, and mobile accessibility.

## Key decisions & tradeoffs

- **Featured placement, NOT paid ranking.** The one irreversible move. Rejected deliberately.
- **Independence is the moat.** Any competitor can outspend SCNM; none can retroactively claim nobody paid to be listed.
- **Services sell as Slab Savvy CPA** — Nathan's call, over a recommendation for a non-CPA name, now gated on G1. Two independent reviews flagged the same risk.
- **Tax work goes to the employer on purpose** — the referral fee makes the incentive point the right way.
- **v1 is deliberately one surface.** Breadth is what makes "doesn't affect organic" unverifiable.
- **Barter is a sales-friction tool only** — no tax advantage, and it adds a second valuation to defend.

## Risks / open questions

- **G1 unresolved** — highest-consequence item; cheap to resolve; blocks everything.
- **No pricing exists** for Featured or services.
- **Capacity — now controlled, not just noted.** Launch as a **pilot capped at a fixed number of concurrent service clients** (Nathan to set the number), with a written service scope, stated response times, and a waitlist once the cap is reached. Selling beyond the cap is out of scope. The underlying risk stands: a full-time KRP role plus DMC, SlabBook, PennyPilot, CoinMyCard and SSC, and nothing here reduces existing load.
- **Three relationships with one shop owner** — ranker, supplier, trading partner. Disclosure is the only control currently designed.
- **Featured's value depends on Google traffic** SCNM does not control.
- **Conflict-of-interest surface grows with every client** — mitigated by the step-14 policy, but the policy is unwritten until someone writes it.
- **Services pilot cap: played by ear** (Nathan, 2026-07-31) — a judgment call rather than a configured limit. The capacity risk below is therefore uncontrolled by design; it rests on him saying no in the moment.
- **Featured slot cap: 1 per city** — set.
- **G1/G4 removed** — the CPA-designation exposure is accepted, not eliminated.

## Out of scope

- Paid ranking in any form.
- Accounting or tax services through SSC.
- Accounting firms as consulting clients while at KRP.
- Changes to the 20-review bar, Bayesian ranking, or volume weighting.
- US expansion.
- Any code before Nathan's sign-off.
